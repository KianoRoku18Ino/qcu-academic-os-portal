CHAPTER 01
Meaning & Scope of Globalization

Before naming what makes globalization happen, the module first pins down what the word itself covers.

📖
Definition (as given)

Globalization refers to the increasing interconnectedness and interdependence of countries, economies, societies, cultures, institutions, and individuals across geographical boundaries. It makes events in one part of the world increasingly capable of affecting people and organizations in another.

What Moves Across Borders

The module names seven distinct things that globalization moves across borders:

  • Goods and services
  • Capital and investments
  • People and labor
  • Technology
  • Information and knowledge
  • Ideas and culture
  • Political and social practices

The Smartphone Example

The module's own illustration: a smartphone used in the Philippines may involve design from the United States, components from South Korea, Taiwan, Japan, and other countries, manufacturing in China, Vietnam, India, or elsewhere, software from international teams, marketing through global advertising campaigns, and consumers around the world. This demonstrates how production, technology, investment, communication, and consumption have all become globally interconnected.

Two Supply-Chain Diagrams the Module Includes

Two sourced diagrams back up the smartphone example with real supply-chain data:

🎤
Recitation-Ready Answer

"Globalization is the growing interconnectedness of countries, economies, societies, and people across borders — it moves goods, capital, people, technology, information, ideas, and political/social practices." Closest related idea: internationalization just means more cross-border activity between separate national economies; globalization goes further by treating the world as one integrated system where a single product (like a smartphone) can be designed, sourced, built, and sold across a dozen countries at once. Example: a Filipino-bought smartphone is designed in the US, built from Korean/Taiwanese/Japanese parts, assembled in China, and sold through global marketing.

CHAPTER 02
Technology, Internet & Social Media

Facilitators of globalization are the forces, technologies, institutions, policies, and developments that make global interconnectedness possible and accelerate the movement of people, goods, capital, information, and ideas — the module's own definition, which applies to all 13 facilitators across this and the next four chapters. This chapter covers Facilitators 1, 2, and 13: the technology cluster.

Facilitator 1 — Technological Advancement

Technology is one of the most important facilitators of globalization. The module names nine specific developments: information technology, artificial intelligence, cloud computing, smartphones, digital platforms, robotics, automation, satellites, and internet infrastructure — all of which have dramatically reduced the time and cost of communicating and conducting business across borders.

💡
Example

A Filipino employee can work remotely for a company based in Australia while communicating through Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, Slack, email, and cloud-based systems — worker and employer never need to be in the same country.

🇵🇭
Philippine Example

The Philippine BPO (Business Process Outsourcing) industry demonstrates this directly — Filipino workers provide customer service, technical support, accounting, animation, software development, and other services to international clients.

Key idea (as stated): Technology reduces the importance of geographical distance.

Facilitator 2 — Internet and Digital Communication

Before widespread internet access, international communication depended heavily on letters, telegrams, telephone calls, fax machines, and physical documents. Today, people communicate internationally within seconds.

A STUDENT IN THE PHILIPPINES CAN
Access & Collaborate
Attend an online lecture by a professor abroad · access international journals · watch educational videos from another country · collaborate with foreign students · participate in international conferences.
A BUSINESS CAN
Trade & Transact
Advertise internationally · sell products online · communicate with suppliers · receive international payments · provide online services.

Facilitator 13 — Social Media

Social media has accelerated globalization by allowing ordinary individuals — not only governments and corporations — to participate in global communication. Platforms let people share ideas, create international communities, promote products, discuss global issues, build international careers, and participate in social movements.

🌐
Important Concept (as stated)

Globalization is no longer driven exclusively by governments and corporations. Individuals are now active participants in globalization.

➕
Added Illustrative Example — Not in the Original Slides

The module describes this concept but doesn't attach a worked example the way it does for most other facilitators. A concrete illustration: a Filipino content creator building an international following on YouTube or TikTok, monetizing through global ad revenue and brand partnerships — participating in globalization with no government or corporate backing required.

CHAPTER 03
Transportation & International Trade

Facilitators 3 and 4 — the physical movement of goods, and the trade relationships that movement makes possible.

Facilitator 3 — Transportation

Modern transportation facilitates the physical movement of people and products across countries. The module names seven developments: container ships, cargo aircraft, high-speed rail, modern highways, international airports, logistics networks, and automated warehouses.

🚢
Example & Global Supply Chain

A product manufactured in China can be shipped to the Philippines and distributed via local logistics companies. The module's general supply-chain flow: Raw materials → Manufacturing → Assembly → Shipping → Distribution → Retail → Consumer — an international production network.

Importance of transportation (as listed): reduces delivery time · lowers transportation costs · expands international trade · facilitates tourism · supports migration · connects producers and consumers.

Facilitator 4 — International Trade

International trade is a major driver of globalization: countries exchange goods and services because no country can efficiently produce everything its population needs.

THE PHILIPPINES IMPORTS

🔗
Trade Creates Interdependence

When countries increasingly depend on one another for goods and services, their economies become interconnected. Example given: a disruption in semiconductor production in one country can affect automobile and electronics production in many other countries.

CHAPTER 05
Liberalization & Trade Agreements

Facilitators 7 and 8 — the policy side of globalization: loosening restrictions, and the formal agreements that carry that loosening out.

➕
Added Gloss — Distinguishing Facilitators 7 & 8 (see Verification Notes #4a)

The module doesn't explicitly separate these two, but they're not the same thing: liberalization is the general policy stance of reducing restrictions; trade agreements/policies are the actual signed instruments governments use to carry that stance out. Liberalization is the goal; agreements are the mechanism.

Facilitator 7 — Liberalization of Trade and Investment

Liberalization refers to reducing restrictions on international trade and investment. Examples of what gets reduced: tariffs, import restrictions, investment restrictions, trade barriers, and foreign ownership restrictions in certain sectors.

⚙️
Example

If a country lowers tariffs on imported machinery, businesses may obtain foreign equipment at lower prices. This can increase international trade, encourage foreign investment, increase competition, and provide consumers with more choices. The module also notes the flip side: liberalization can create challenges for local businesses that must now compete with large international companies.

Facilitator 8 — Global Economic Policies and Trade Agreements

Governments facilitate globalization through agreements that promote international economic cooperation: free trade agreements, regional economic partnerships, bilateral trade agreements, and multilateral trade agreements.

🌏
ASEAN Example

The Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) promotes regional economic cooperation and integration. Its initiatives encourage trade, investment, tourism, regional production, labor cooperation, and economic integration. Philippine Context: the Philippines participates in ASEAN economic cooperation, connecting Filipino businesses and workers to broader regional markets.

➕
Added for Context — Not in the Original Slides (verified separately)

The module doesn't give ASEAN's founding date. Verified: ASEAN was founded on 8 August 1967 in Bangkok via the ASEAN Declaration (Bangkok Declaration), signed by five founding members — Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, and Thailand.

CHAPTER 07
Development & Structural Theories

The first three theories all ask a version of the same question — does global integration lift countries up, or lock some of them into a disadvantaged position? Theories, remember, are the module's interpretations layered on top of the facilitators covered in Chapters 02–06.

Theory 1 — Modernization Theory

Modernization theory argues that societies develop through a process of transformation from traditional societies toward modern, industrial, technologically advanced societies. Main idea: Traditional society → Industrialization → Urbanization → Technological development → Modern society. Technology, education, industrialization, and economic development are viewed as the key forces of modernization.

🌾
Example

A rural community gradually adopts internet access, digital banking, modern agriculture, e-commerce, and online education — increasing its integration with the broader national and global economy.

⚠️
Criticism (as stated)

Critics argue that modernization theory may assume that Western countries represent the ideal model of development.

Theory 2 — Dependency Theory

Dependency theory provides a more critical explanation: poorer countries may remain economically dependent on wealthier countries because of unequal international economic relationships. Basic structure: Core countries → Periphery countries. Core countries tend to possess capital, technology, powerful corporations, and financial institutions. Peripheral countries may provide raw materials, labor, agricultural products, and low-cost manufacturing.

🌍
Example & Philippine Example

A developing country may export raw materials at relatively low prices while importing high-value manufactured products — creating an unequal economic relationship. Philippine Example: the Philippines may export labor and certain raw materials while importing technologically advanced products.

❓
Key Question (as stated)

Does globalization create equal opportunities for all countries, or does it reproduce existing inequalities?

Theory 3 — World-Systems Theory

World-systems theory, associated particularly with Immanuel Wallerstein, views the global economy as a single interconnected system, divided into three categories:

CORE
Advanced & Capital-Rich
Advanced industries · strong financial systems · high levels of capital · technological advantages.
SEMI-PERIPHERY
Intermediate Position
Developing industries · growing economies · significant manufacturing · increasing international influence.
PERIPHERY
Resource-Dependent
Depends heavily on raw-material exports, low-cost labor, and external investment.

Simplified model: CORE ↔ SEMI-PERIPHERY ↔ PERIPHERY. The categories are not necessarily permanent — countries can change their position over time. Comparison-table example (Ch. 10): "international division of labor."

➕
Added Illustrative Scenario — Not in the Original Slides

The module gives the three-tier structure but no single narrative example the way it does for Modernization or Dependency theory. A concrete illustration: Vietnam assembling smartphones and garments for global brands places it in a semi-periphery role — more industrialized than a purely raw-material exporter, but still importing high-end components and technology from core countries like the US, Japan, and South Korea.

CHAPTER 08
State-Centered Perspectives

Theories 4–7 all argue about the same question from different angles: is the nation-state losing power to globalization, holding steady, or just changing shape?

Theory 4 — Globalization Theory (General)

Globalization theory focuses on the increasing interconnectedness of societies through: technology, markets, communication, migration, culture, politics, and international institutions. It views globalization as a process that increasingly connects local activities to global processes.

🛍️
Example

A local Filipino business selling handmade products through an international e-commerce platform can reach customers in Japan, the United States, Australia, Singapore, and Europe — a local economic activity becoming connected to a global market.

Theory 5 — Hyperglobalist Perspective

The hyperglobalist perspective argues that globalization is fundamentally transforming the world: national borders are becoming less important, global markets are becoming increasingly powerful, multinational corporations have significant influence, technology is creating a highly integrated global economy, and states may lose some traditional economic control.

🏭
Example

A company can design a product in one country, manufacture it in another, market it globally, sell it through an international platform, and receive digital payments from customers worldwide.

❓
Key Question (as stated)

Is globalization creating a world where national economies are becoming increasingly integrated into one global market?

Theory 6 — Skeptical Perspective

The skeptical perspective questions the idea that globalization is completely new or that national borders have become irrelevant. Skeptics argue: states remain powerful, regional economies remain important, international trade has existed for centuries, globalization may be exaggerated, and economic integration is uneven.

🏛️
Example

Even when international companies operate in a country, the government still controls laws, taxes, immigration, regulation, public policy, and national security. Therefore, nation-states continue to have substantial power.

Theory 7 — Transformationalist Perspective

The transformationalist perspective sits in the middle: globalization is real and powerful, but it does not simply eliminate the nation-state. Instead, globalization transforms government, business, society, culture, institutions, and political authority.

🌐
Example

Governments today must consider international trade, cybersecurity, climate change, migration, financial markets, public health, and technology. The state remains important, but its role changes.

Simple formula (as stated): Globalization does not necessarily eliminate the state; it transforms the way the state operates.

CHAPTER 09
Cultural & Network Theories

Theories 8–12 turn to culture and information networks — what happens to identity, taste, and social organization once everything is connected.

Theory 8 — Cultural Globalization Theory

Cultural globalization focuses on the worldwide movement and interaction of: values, beliefs, languages, food, fashion, music, films, entertainment, and lifestyles.

📱
Example

A Filipino teenager may simultaneously consume American films, Korean dramas, Japanese anime, Filipino music, European football, and global social media trends — creating a cultural environment where local and foreign influences interact.

Theory 9 — Homogenization Theory

Cultural homogenization suggests that globalization may cause cultures to become increasingly similar. Global brands, entertainment, consumer products, and lifestyles can create common patterns of consumption.

🍔
Example

People in different countries may consume the same fast food, clothing brands, smartphones, movies, music, and social media platforms.

⚠️
Concern (as stated)

Critics argue that excessive cultural homogenization could weaken local traditions, indigenous cultures, local languages, and traditional practices.

Theory 10 — Glocalization Theory

Glocalization combines Global + Local: global products, services, or ideas are adapted to local cultures and preferences.

🍚
Example

An international fast-food company may modify its menu for Filipino preferences — introducing rice meals, local flavors, and regionally appropriate products. The global company remains global, but its products are adapted locally.

Key concept (as stated): Think globally, act locally.

Theory 11 — Hybridization Theory

Cultural hybridization argues that globalization does not simply replace local cultures with global cultures — instead, different cultural elements combine to create something new.

🇵🇭
Example

Filipino culture may combine Filipino + American + Korean + Japanese + Spanish influences, producing new forms of music, fashion, food, language, entertainment, and lifestyle. For instance, Filipino social media content may combine local humor with global memes and international trends.

Theory 12 — Network Society Theory

Associated with sociologist Manuel Castells, network society theory emphasizes the importance of information and communication networks. Modern societies increasingly operate through networks involving digital communication, information, technology, organizations, markets, and individuals.

🔗
Example

A business may operate through a network: Supplier → Digital platform → Logistics provider → Payment system → Consumer. None of these participants necessarily needs to be located in the same country.

➕
Added for Context — Not in the Original Slides (verified separately)

The module gives no date. Castells introduced network society theory in his 1996 book The Rise of the Network Society, the first volume of his Information Age trilogy.

Key Takeaway (as stated)

Globalization does not happen by itself. It is facilitated by technology, transportation, trade, communication, multinational corporations, migration, international institutions, education, finance, and cultural exchange. At the same time, globalization can be understood differently depending on the theoretical perspective used:

  • Modernization theory emphasizes development.
  • Dependency theory emphasizes inequality.
  • World-systems theory emphasizes the structure of the global economy.
  • Hyperglobalism emphasizes increasing global integration.
  • Skepticism emphasizes the continuing power of states.
  • Transformationalism emphasizes how globalization changes institutions and societies.
  • Cultural theories explain how global and local cultures interact through homogenization, glocalization, and hybridization.
