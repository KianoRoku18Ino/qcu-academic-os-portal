Nation, State & the Six Metaphors of Globalization

Core Distinctions (Always Tested)

ConceptDefinitionKey ClueExample

NationPeople bound by common history, culture, emotional/psychological bonds"shared identity," "cultural heritage," "no homeland"Kurds, Palestinians

⚠️
Trap

A nation does NOT need territory or sovereignty. A state DOES.

Montevideo Convention (1933)

Signed: December 26, 1933. In force: December 26, 1934.

Four essential elements of a state: 1. Permanent Population 2. Defined Territory 3. Government 4. Capacity to Enter Relations with Other States (Sovereignty).

Scenario clue: "A group of people in a defined area with their own government and the ability to trade with other countries" is describing a STATE under Montevideo.

World State Count (Verified)

  • 195 states total
  • 193 UN member states
  • 2 non-members: Holy See (Vatican) and Palestine

The Six Metaphors of Globalization HIGH PRIORITY

MetaphorDefinitionKey Clue
FlowLifting barriers, easing rules on commerce, human movement, communication"global trade grew," "cheaper communication"
FrictionConflict, disparity, crisis from the open market"job losses," "US-China trade war," "1% vs 99%"
AccelerationExponential growth of tech capacity"AI," "global warming," "data security"
DisruptionStartup innovations outperform branded products"Uber, Netflix, Airbnb"
DislocationThose who cannot keep pace get displaced"automation replaces jobs," "small firms eaten by big business"
ConnectivityInfrastructure boosting global competitiveness"airports, bridges," "education, internet"
Flow
ARGUMENTS
Global trade grew several-fold. Many lifted from poverty (China, India). Regional trade agreements: EU, ASEAN, NAFTA (now USMCA), APEC. Internet democratized communication.
Friction
ARGUMENTS
Job markets weakened in advanced economies. US-China trade war. Al-Qaeda and ISIS exploit the borderless world. 1% vs 99% income gap. Racial slurs in America. Cyberwar as the 5th domain.
Acceleration
ARGUMENTS
AI evolution. New jobs require new skills. Can machines outperform humans? Less petroleum dependence. Data security risk. Global warming needs US-China cooperation.
Disruption
EXAMPLES
Uber, Netflix, Airbnb, driverless cars. China is the biggest disruption: communism paired with market capitalism.
Dislocation
ARGUMENTS
1st-3rd Industrial Revolution jobs replaced by automation. Small firms eaten by big businesses. Local markets hurt by cheap foreign goods.
Connectivity
ARGUMENTS
Singapore Changi Airport, best in the world. China's One Belt, One Road. Best education systems match the most innovative economies.
Scenario clue: "A local taxi company loses customers to a ride-hailing app" is Disruption.
Scenario clue: "Factory workers lose jobs to robots" is Dislocation.
Scenario clue: "A new airport boosts a country's trade competitiveness" is Connectivity.

4IR x Globalization

➡️
Chain Reaction

Technology, then Faster Communication, then Lower Transaction Costs, then Global Business Networks, then Greater International Economic Integration.

How the 4IR changes globalization: more digital (adds Data, Algorithms, Digital Services, Knowledge); remote work (a Filipino working for a foreign company); global e-commerce (MSMEs reach international customers); smart manufacturing (Sensors to Data to AI to Automated machines to Real-time decisions); changing employment (some jobs automated, new jobs in AI, cybersecurity, data analytics).

BENEFITS
What the 4IR Gets Right

Higher productivity, improved innovation, better decision-making, improved customer experience, greater connectivity, new business opportunities, potential improvement in quality of life.

CHALLENGES
Where It Gets Risky

Job displacement, digital divide, cybersecurity, data privacy, ethical problems in AI (bias, discrimination, misinformation), inequality.

MODULE 4
Global Interstate System & Global Governance

Global Interstate System

Definition: the system of relationships among independent and sovereign states. Structure: STATE A ↔ STATE B ↔ STATE C ↔ STATE D.

DOMESTIC
Government to Laws to Citizens

The government has enforcement authority.

INTERNATIONAL
No Single World Government

No comprehensive authority above all states.

The State as Basic Actor (Same 4 Elements as Module 1)

  1. Population, people permanently living in the territory
  2. Territory, defined geographical area
  3. Government, political institutions exercising authority
  4. Sovereignty, supreme authority within territory plus external relations

Key Concepts of the Interstate System

ConceptDefinitionClue
SovereigntyAuthority over internal affairs, legal independence"non-interference"
AnarchyNO single supreme world authority (NOT chaos)"no world government"
National InterestSecurity, economic development, territorial integrity"states pursue their interests"
Balance of PowerPrevent one state from becoming overwhelmingly dominant"alliances," "counterbalance"
DiplomacyNegotiation, dialogue, mediation, treaty-making"peaceful resolution"
⚠️
Trap

"Anarchy" in international relations does NOT mean chaos. It means there is no single supreme world authority above sovereign states.

Scenario clue: "Country A is very powerful; Countries B and C form an alliance to counterbalance A" is Balance of Power.
Scenario clue: "Two countries disagree over fishing rights and hold bilateral meetings instead of using force" is Diplomacy.

Global Governance

Definition: processes through which different actors cooperate to address problems that transcend national borders. Key point: Global Governance is NOT World Government.

GLOBAL GOVERNANCE
What It Is

No single global government. Multiple actors. Cooperation and coordination.

WORLD GOVERNMENT
What It Is NOT

One centralized world government. One supreme political authority. Centralized rule.

Who Participates

  1. States, remain important, hold sovereignty
  2. International Organizations, UN, WHO, IMF, World Bank, WTO
  3. Regional Organizations, ASEAN, EU, African Union, OAS
  4. NGOs, human rights, environment, poverty, humanitarian aid
  5. Multinational Corporations, influence employment, investment, trade
  6. Civil Society, advocacy organizations, professional associations

ChallengeProblem

How the Interstate System & Global Governance Work Together

1
States have different interests
This is the Global Interstate System.
2

This is International Diplomacy.
3

This becomes part of Global Governance.
4

This strengthens Global Governance.
5
Businesses, NGOs, scientists, communities participate
Global Governance becomes broader than state-to-state relations.

Exam Strategy Tips
1
Read the scenario carefully for clue words
"Shared culture/history" is Nation. "Defined territory + sovereignty" is State. "Startup beats established brand" is Disruption. "Workers replaced by machines" is Dislocation. "Raw materials vs manufactured goods" is Dependency Theory. "Adapts global product to local taste" is Glocalization. "Cultures combine to form new" is Hybridization. "No single world authority" is Anarchy (IR sense). "Multiple actors cooperate" is Global Governance.
2
Eliminate obviously wrong answers first
Many distractors are pulled from a different module than the question is testing.
3
Watch for dates
GATT (1947/48) vs WTO (1995) is a classic trap.
4
Know the theorists
Wallerstein (World-Systems), Castells (Network Society), Giddens (4 dimensions), Friedman (Flat World).
5
Anarchy does not equal chaos
In international relations, it just means no single supreme world authority.
6
Global Governance does not equal World Government
This is a key distinction the exam likes to test directly.
