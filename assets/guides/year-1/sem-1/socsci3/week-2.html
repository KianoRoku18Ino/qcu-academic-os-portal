CHAPTER 01
Dimensions & Components of Globalization

Globalization is more than goods moving between countries — it's a complex system of economic, political, technological, social, cultural, and institutional connections linking societies across borders. Classic example from the lesson: a smartphone designed in one country, built from parts made in several others, assembled elsewhere, sold on a global digital platform, and bought by a Filipino consumer.

The Six Dimensions

DimensionCovers
EconomicInternational trade, foreign investment, global production, multinational corporations.
PoliticalInternational organizations, treaties, diplomatic cooperation.
TechnologicalInternet, telecommunications, digital platforms, AI.
CulturalGlobal spread of music, films, food, fashion, languages, lifestyles.
SocialMigration, international education, tourism, social networks.
EnvironmentalClimate change, pollution, biodiversity, and other cross-border problems.

The 8 Major Components of Globalization's Structure

1. Global Markets

Businesses can sell internationally, buy from foreign suppliers, employ workers abroad, receive foreign investment, and advertise on global platforms. Example: a small Filipino clothing business reaching customers worldwide through Facebook, TikTok, Shopee, Lazada, or its own site — technology erases the old geographic limits.

2. International Trade

Countries trade because they differ in natural resources, labor costs, technology, climate, production capability, and consumer preferences. The Philippines exports agricultural products, electronics, and BPO services, and imports petroleum, machinery, electronic components, and food. The WTO traces international economic integration back to networks like the Silk Road, through GATT, to the modern WTO.

3. Global Supply Chains

A production process split across countries: Design (US) → Components (South Korea/Japan/Taiwan/China) → Assembly (China/Vietnam) → Shipping (international logistics) → Marketing (global platforms) → Consumer (Philippines). Advantage: cheaper materials, specialized labor, advanced tech, bigger markets. Disadvantage: a disruption in one country can ripple out to affect everyone downstream.

4. Multinational Corporations (MNCs)

Companies operating in multiple countries, moving capital, technology, management, products, and knowledge across borders. Example structure: HQ in the US, R&D in Europe, manufacturing in Asia, customer service in the Philippines, customers worldwide.

5. Global Financial System

The cross-border movement of money: foreign direct investment, international banking, stock markets, remittances, foreign exchange, international loans, portfolio investments. Example: a major economy raising interest rates can ripple into currency values, investment flows, borrowing costs, stock markets, and developing economies elsewhere.

6. International Institutions

WTO
TRADE RULES
World Trade Organization
Established January 1, 1995, succeeding GATT. Unlike GATT (goods only), WTO agreements also cover services and intellectual property.
IMF
MONETARY STABILITY
International Monetary Fund
Promotes international monetary cooperation and financial stability.
World Bank
DEVELOPMENT FINANCE
Financing & development support
Provides financing and development support to countries.
UN
GLOBAL COOPERATION
United Nations
Facilitates international cooperation on peace, development, human rights, and other global concerns.

7. Technology & Communication

The transformation chain: Letters → Telephone → Radio → Television → Internet → Smartphone → AI. Example: a Filipino student attending an online class with an international professor, watching foreign educational content, collaborating with overseas students, or working remotely for an international company. Digital technology has reduced the importance of physical distance.

8. Global Migration

People cross borders for employment, education, family, business, tourism, or humanitarian reasons. For the Philippines, OFWs are the prime example of globalization connecting labor markets.

MIGRATION
Benefits

Employment opportunities · remittances · skills transfer · international networks.

MIGRATION
Challenges

Family separation · brain drain · labor exploitation · cultural adjustment.

🎤
Recitation-Ready Answer

"Globalization's structure has eight moving parts: global markets, international trade, global supply chains, multinational corporations, the global financial system, international institutions like the WTO and IMF, technology and communication, and global migration." If pressed for the sharpest Philippine example: OFWs for migration, and the smartphone supply chain (design in the US, parts from Asia, assembly in China/Vietnam, sold to a Filipino consumer) for supply chains.

CHAPTER 02
History of the Modern Global Economy

The modern global economy didn't appear overnight — it developed through several historical stages, and globalization isn't a straight line: it can expand, and it can reverse.

StagePeriodWhat Happened
1. Early Global TradeAncient eraThe Silk Road connected Asia, the Middle East, Europe, exchanging silk, spices, precious metals, textiles, agricultural goods — and transmitting religions, technologies, languages, and science along with them.
2. Age of Exploration~15th century onwardEuropean maritime exploration expanded trade routes and colonies across Asia, Africa, the Americas — but also brought colonialism, exploitation, slavery, and unequal economic relationships.
3. First Industrial RevolutionLate 18th century, began in BritainSteam power, mechanized production, factories, railways, steamships — dramatically cutting the time/cost of moving goods and people. Chain: factories → more production → more trade → bigger markets → stronger international ties.
4. Second Industrial RevolutionLate 19th–early 20th centuryElectricity, telecommunications, steel, automobiles, modern chemicals — mass production became possible, creating mass markets and mass consumption.
5. World Wars & the Great DepressionInterwar periodWWI, the Great Depression, protectionism, and WWII disrupted economic integration — proof that global integration can reverse, not just expand. Protectionist and retaliatory trade policies sharply contracted world trade during the Depression.
6. Bretton Woods System1944Representatives met in Bretton Woods, New Hampshire to build a postwar framework for economic cooperation — leading to the IMF and the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (which became part of the World Bank Group).
7. GATT1947/194823 countries concluded the initial tariff negotiations in 1947; GATT came into effect provisionally in 1948, aiming for lower tariffs, more trade, predictable rules, and cooperation. It governed most of world trade from 1948–1994.
8. Expansion of TradePostwar era onwardLower trade barriers, better transport, containerization, international agreements, MNCs, and better communication all fueled trade growth. The shipping container (from the 1950s) standardized how goods moved between ships, trains, and trucks.
9. Third Industrial RevolutionMid-20th century onwardComputers, semiconductors, telecommunications, automation, the internet, information systems — enabling information processing and transmission at unprecedented speed.
10. Creation of the WTO1995The Uruguay Round of GATT negotiations (1986–94) produced the Marrakesh Agreement, signed April 15, 1994; the WTO began operating January 1, 1995, expanding coverage to goods, services, intellectual property, and dispute settlement.
11. 21st-Century Globalization2000s–presentGlobalization becomes increasingly digital, networked, knowledge-based, data-driven, and technology-intensive.
📌
GATT → WTO in one line

GATT (1947–1994) was a provisional agreement focused mainly on goods trade with limited dispute resolution. The WTO (1995–present) is a permanent institution with an expanded mandate covering goods (GATT 1994), services (GATS), and intellectual property (TRIPS) — plus a stronger, binding dispute-settlement system.

🎤
Recitation-Ready Answer

If asked to sequence the key milestones: "Silk Road → Age of Exploration → First and Second Industrial Revolutions → World Wars/Depression → Bretton Woods (1944) → GATT (1947/48) → Third Industrial Revolution → WTO (1995) → 21st-century digital globalization." The one non-negotiable date pair to memorize: GATT 1947/48, WTO 1995.

CHAPTER 03
The Fourth Industrial Revolution

The Fourth Industrial Revolution (4IR) is the current wave of technological transformation defined by the integration of digital, physical, and biological technologies. The World Economic Forum describes it as building on the digital revolution, fusing technologies that blur the boundaries between physical, digital, and biological systems.

🔁
In Simple Terms

3rd Industrial Revolution = Computers + Internet + Automation. 4th Industrial Revolution = AI + Robotics + IoT + Big Data + Biotechnology + Advanced Computing + Automation.

The Four Industrial Revolutions at a Glance

RevolutionApprox. PeriodMajor TechnologyMain Economic Effect
1stLate 18th–19th centurySteam, mechanizationFactory production
2ndLate 19th–early 20th centuryElectricity, assembly lineMass production
3rd20th centuryComputers, electronics, InternetAutomation and digitalization
4th21st centuryAI, robotics, IoT, biotechnologyIntelligent and interconnected systems

7 Major Technologies of the 4IR

Artificial Intelligence
4IR TECH 1
Systems that do human-like tasks
ChatGPT/generative AI, image & speech recognition, recommendation systems, predictive analytics, autonomous systems. Business example: a restaurant using AI to forecast demand, analyze preferences, automate inquiries, personalize marketing, and optimize inventory.
Internet of Things (IoT)
4IR TECH 2
Physical objects that talk to the internet
Smart watches, smart homes, connected vehicles, industrial sensors, smart factories. Example: a factory machine reporting "Temperature is increasing — maintenance may be required" for predictive maintenance.
Robotics & Automation
4IR TECH 3
Machines doing the repetitive or risky work
Industrial robots, warehouse robots, surgical robots, autonomous vehicles, agricultural robots. Increases productivity but changes what skills workers need.
Big Data & Analytics
4IR TECH 4
Turning huge datasets into decisions
Understanding customer behavior, purchase patterns, market trends, operations, risk. Example: an online retailer spotting "customers who buy Product A also buy Product B" to power recommendations.
Cloud Computing
4IR TECH 5
Infrastructure without owning the hardware
Access to storage, applications, computing power, and databases over the internet instead of maintaining everything locally.
Blockchain
4IR TECH 6
A transparent, hard-to-tamper-with ledger
A distributed digital ledger designed for transparency and resistance to unauthorized alteration. Potential uses: financial transactions, supply-chain tracking, digital identity, smart contracts, records management.
Biotechnology
4IR TECH 7
The 4IR's biological side
Genetic engineering, genome editing, personalized medicine, synthetic biology — identified by the World Economic Forum among core 4IR-associated technologies.
🎤
Recitation-Ready Answer

"The 4IR runs on seven core technologies: AI, IoT, robotics and automation, big data, cloud computing, blockchain, and biotechnology — and what makes it different from the 3rd Industrial Revolution is that these technologies increasingly converge and interact with each other, not just operate side by side."

CHAPTER 04
4IR × Globalization
➡️
The Chain Reaction

Technology → Faster Communication → Lower Transaction Costs → Global Business Networks → Greater International Economic Integration.

5 Ways the 4IR Changes Globalization

1
Globalization becomes more digital
Traditional globalization moved Goods + Capital + People. Digital globalization adds Data + Algorithms + Digital Services + Knowledge — a software company can sell internationally without ever shipping a physical product.
2
Remote work
A Filipino professional in Quezon City can work for an American, Australian, Singaporean, or European employer they may never meet in person.
3
Global e-commerce
Digital platforms let a small entrepreneur reach international customers without opening physical stores abroad — opportunity for MSMEs, freelancers, content creators, online sellers, digital entrepreneurs.
4
Smart manufacturing
Traditional factory: Human workers → machines → production. Smart factory (Industry 4.0): Sensors → Data → AI → Automated machines → Real-time decisions.
5
Changing nature of employment
Some jobs get automated, redesigned, or AI-augmented — but new roles emerge in AI, cybersecurity, data analytics, robotics, cloud computing, digital marketing, and software development. The real question isn't "will tech eliminate jobs" but "what skills will workers need."

Benefits vs. Challenges

7 BENEFITS
What the 4IR Gets Right

Higher productivity · improved innovation · better real-time decision-making · improved customer experience/personalization · greater global connectivity/collaboration · new business opportunities (lower barriers to global markets) · potential quality-of-life gains in healthcare, education, transportation, agriculture, communication, public services.

6 CHALLENGES
Where It Gets Risky

Job displacement (routine tasks most at risk) · digital divide (unequal access to internet/devices/education) · cybersecurity threats · data privacy (who owns/accesses/protects your data) · ethical problems in AI (bias, discrimination, misinformation, accountability) · inequality (advanced economies and skilled workers can capture more of the gains).

Globalization 4.0

A useful lens for the relationship between globalization and the 4IR — four eras building on each other:

EraDefined By
Globalization 1.0Countries and physical exploration.
Globalization 2.0Industrialization and mass production.
Globalization 3.0Digitalization and global networks.
Globalization 4.0AI + automation + data + biotechnology + interconnected intelligent systems.

The World Economic Forum frames the 4IR as driving this new phase of globalization, where technological change, global systems, standards, and institutions interact in new ways.

🎤
Recitation-Ready Answer

"The 4IR changes globalization in five ways: it makes it more digital, enables remote work, powers global e-commerce, drives smart manufacturing, and reshapes what jobs even look like. It brings real benefits — productivity, innovation, connectivity — but also real risks: job displacement, the digital divide, and growing inequality between who can access the technology and who can't."

5 Key Takeaways

  1. Globalization connects economies — countries increasingly depend on each other for goods, services, capital, technology, information, and labor.
  2. The modern global economy developed historically — shaped by industrialization, the world wars, the Great Depression, Bretton Woods, GATT, technology, and the WTO.
  3. Technology accelerates globalization — transportation and communication tech have continually shrunk the barrier of distance.
  4. The 4IR is different because of technological convergence — AI, robotics, IoT, big data, and biotechnology increasingly interact with each other.
  5. Globalization creates both opportunities and challenges — the goal isn't just to embrace technology, but to make its benefits inclusive, ethical, sustainable, and accessible.
