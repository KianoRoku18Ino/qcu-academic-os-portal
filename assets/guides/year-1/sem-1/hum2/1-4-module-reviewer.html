CHAPTER 01
Module 1: Basic Concepts

Focus: what ethics is (the wrongness and correctness of our actions) and how it differs from look-alike ideas.

What Is Ethics?

KEYWORDS: wrongness, correctness, actions
Ethics
Focus: right and wrong of what we do
Lecture: The wrongness and correctness of our actions.
Scenario: Asking "Is it right to copy a classmate's exam answers?" is an ethics question.
KEYWORDS: Greek, characteristic way of acting, Latin, tradition, custom
Ethos and Mores
Focus: word origin
Lecture: Ethos (Greek) means "characteristic way of acting." Its Latin equivalent is mos, mores, meaning tradition or custom.
KEYWORDS: philo = love, sophia = wisdom, "Why?", wondering, Plato
Philosophy
Focus: the bigger field that ethics belongs to
Lecture: From the Greek philo (love) and sophia (wisdom), literally "the love of wisdom." It is the study of the most basic and profound matters of human existence, fundamental questions that ask "Why?" "It begins with wondering." (Plato)

The Four Main Areas of Ethics

KEYWORDS: empirical research, attitudes, beliefs, what people think
Descriptive Ethics
Focus: what people actually believe
Lecture: Empirical research into the attitudes of individuals or groups of people. It aims to uncover people's beliefs about values, which actions are right and wrong, and which characteristics of moral agents are virtuous.
Lecture example: Surveys of ethical attitudes among employees and managers, such as whether they feel pressure to compromise moral principles to achieve organizational goals.
Scenario: A school surveys 300 students on whether copying homework is okay. It only reports what they believe.
KEYWORDS: criteria, morally right and wrong, standards justified, deontological, teleological or consequentialist
Normative Ethics
Focus: what is right, and why the standard is justified
Lecture: Concerned with criteria of what is morally right and wrong. Central question: how are basic moral standards arrived at and justified? Answers fall into two broad categories: deontological and teleological/consequentialist.
Lecture example: "It is wrong to kill people just because they make you angry." / "Pain is intrinsically bad, so we ought not cause pain without a good reason."
Scenario: "Employees ought not to accept gifts that could bias their decisions." This sets a standard.
KEYWORDS: nature, "What is goodness?", "What is morality?", how to identify good or bad
Metaethics
Focus: the meaning and nature of moral ideas
Lecture: Seeks to understand the nature of ethical properties, principles, judgments, and attitudes. It tries to answer "What is morality?", "What is goodness?", and "How to identify if something is good or bad?"
Scenario: A group is not arguing whether one act is wrong. They are asking what the word "good" itself means.
KEYWORDS: professional, disciplinary, practical field, medical, business, legal
Applied Ethics
Focus: ethics inside a specific field
Lecture: Deals with ethical questions specific to a professional, disciplinary, or practical field. Subsets include medical ethics, bioethics, business ethics, legal ethics, and others.
Scenario: A hospital asks whether patients may be used for medical tests. That is a field question (biomedical).
Applied ethics subfieldSample question from the lecture
Animal EthicsIs it permissible to eat meat?
Biomedical EthicsOn whom are we allowed to perform medical tests?
Business EthicsDo corporations have moral status?
Environmental EthicsOught we curb climate change for the sake of future generations?
Information EthicsMay I pirate music?
LawShould personal, recreational drug use be illegal?
Philosophy of the FamilyWhat do children owe their parents?
Practical Distributive JusticeTo what extent ought we give to charity?
Procreative EthicsIs it permissible to abort a fetus?
Sexual EthicsShould prostitution be legal?
✓
Memory trick

Descriptive = what people BELIEVE. Normative = what is RIGHT. Metaethics = what does "right" MEAN. Applied = ethics in a FIELD.

Ethics vs Morals

KEYWORDS: specific beliefs or attitudes, acts people perform, personal conduct
Morals
Focus: the actual belief or act
Lecture: On specific beliefs or attitudes that people have, or to describe acts that people perform: personal conduct.
Scenario: "I personally believe cheating is wrong." That is a moral stance.
KEYWORDS: discipline, studying, understanding, ideal human behavior, ideal ways of thinking
Ethics (as a discipline)
Focus: the study of behavior
Lecture: The discipline of studying and understanding ideal human behavior and ideal ways of thinking.
Scenario: A class that examines why cheating is wrong is doing ethics.

Kinds of Valuation (Ethics vs Etiquette, Aesthetics, Technique)

KEYWORDS: customary code, polite behavior, group or profession
Etiquette
Focus: politeness
Lecture: Customary code of polite behavior in society or among members of a particular profession or group.
Lecture example: Classroom etiquette: turn off your cell phone, do not pack up before class ends, do not have private conversations.
Scenario: Chewing with your mouth open at a formal dinner is rude, not a moral crime.
KEYWORDS: personal approval, disapproval, individual tastes, preferences
Aesthetics
Focus: taste and beauty
Lecture: Judgments of personal approval and disapproval referring to individual tastes and preferences.
Lecture example: A sunset on the beach is highly beautiful; a plate of food served with elegance; it is "wrong" to wear a leather vest over a Barong Tagalog.
Scenario: Saying a red and neon green outfit "looks wrong" is taste.
KEYWORDS: proper way, right way of doing things
Technique / Technical
Focus: correct procedure
Lecture: The proper way or right way of doing things.
Lecture example: How to bake (it can be "wrong" when it goes against the correct way); how to play basketball; how to shut down a computer.
Scenario: Bread that does not rise because you used the wrong flour to water ratio is a technique error.
!
Only one of the four is a moral matter

All four use the words "right/wrong" or "good/bad." Ethics is about the wrongness and correctness of our actions. Etiquette = polite, Aesthetics = taste, Technique = proper procedure.

Issue, Judgment, Decision, Dilemma

KEYWORDS: controversial, people keep debating
Issue
Focus: a public debate
Lecture: Controversial, and people keep on debating about it.
Lecture example: Cigarette smoking; the anti-terror bill.
Scenario: The country keeps debating whether the death penalty should return.
KEYWORDS: assessment, actions or behavior of someone
Judgment
Focus: evaluating an act (right or wrong)
Lecture: Assessment of the actions or behavior of someone.
Lecture example: A friend chooses to steal from a store and I make an assessment that it is wrong; "Cigarette smoking is dangerous to your health"; "Humility is a virtue."
Scenario: "What she did was dishonest."
KEYWORDS: choice, what to perform or do
Decision
Focus: your own choice of action
Lecture: Choice of what to perform or do.
Lecture example: I choose not to take something I did not pay for; I decided to look for both local and remote employees; I decided to take a vacation in the province.
Scenario: "I will not copy anyone's answers."
KEYWORDS: difficult choice, two or more alternatives, equally undesirable
Dilemma
Focus: stuck between hard options
Lecture: A situation in which a difficult choice has to be made between two or more alternatives, especially equally undesirable ones.
Lecture example: Should Michael reveal to Roger that Phyllis is married? / My mother gave me two choices: do the dishes or clean the bathroom. / Say nothing and just wait to see what happens?
Scenario: You can report your close friend's cheating or keep the secret and let an unfair grade stand. Both choices hurt.

Look-alikes to avoid mixing up

Descriptive vs Normative
Descriptive reports what people believe. Normative says what is right and how the standard is justified.
Ethics vs Morals
Ethics is the discipline of studying ideal behavior. Morals are the specific beliefs or acts of personal conduct.
Issue vs Dilemma
An issue is a topic many people keep debating. A dilemma is a difficult choice between alternatives.
Judgment vs Decision
Judgment assesses someone's action. Decision is the choice of what to perform or do.

Morality vs Law and Religion

MoralityLaw
About what to do in a certain situation.Does not tell us what to do, only what to avoid.
What is moral could be legal.What is legal is not necessarily moral.
KEYWORDS: holy in itself, loved by the gods
Morality vs Religion (Euthyphro exchange)
Focus: is something holy because the gods love it, or loved because it is holy?
Lecture: Plato's Euthyphro. Socrates: "Is it holy in itself and that is why it is loved by the gods?" Religion's answer (Euthyphro): "What is holy is loved by the gods."
Scenario: Is honesty good because a religion commands it, or does the religion command it because honesty is good in itself?

Cultural Relativism

KEYWORDS: no universal truth, cultural codes, nothing more, objectivity and universality challenged
Cultural Relativism (Rachels)
Focus: the CULTURE is the standard
Lecture: It says there is no such thing as universal truth in ethics; there are only the various cultural codes, and nothing more. It challenges our belief in the objectivity and universality of moral truth. Customs cannot be called "correct" or "incorrect" because that implies an independent standard of right and wrong.
Lecture example: Darius and the Callatians (they ate their deceased fathers out of respect, while the Greeks cremated their dead); infanticide among Eskimo groups (lecture wording); female genital mutilation (FGM) in certain communities.
Scenario: A greeting or custom that is respectful in one country is rude in another, and the theory says neither can be called the right one.
APPEAL
Why people like it

  • Conforms to experience: societies really do differ
  • Gives a basis for valuation
  • Promotes tolerance
  • Denies "universal truth": no moral truths for all peoples at all times

CRITICISMS
Problems with it

  • Cannot criticize other cultures (even practices like FGM)
  • No right answers when moral codes conflict
  • "My culture" is unclear (for someone in more than one culture)

Moral Subjectivism

KEYWORDS: individual thinking person, the subject, at the heart of all valuations
Moral Subjectivism
Focus: the INDIVIDUAL is the standard
Lecture: "The individual thinking person (the subject) is at the heart of all valuations." Moral judgments depend on the individual's personal feelings, opinions, or attitudes.
Scenario: "It is good because I say it is good, and nobody can tell me otherwise."
Subjectivist claimCounter-argument (lecture)
"No one can tell me what is right and wrong."We can be mistaken, and we can be corrected by others.
"No one knows my situation better than myself."Many human experiences are common, and others may have something useful to suggest.
"I am entitled to my own opinion."Closed-mindedness rightly invites censure from someone trying to think more critically about values.
"It is good if I say that it is good."What is my basis for saying that it is good?

Psychological vs Ethical Egoism

KEYWORDS: IS claim, descriptive, ego has desires and interests, all actions geared to satisfy
Psychological Egoism
Focus: what people DO
Lecture: "The ego or self has its desires and interests, and all our actions are geared toward satisfying these interests."
Scenario: "Everyone who helps others is secretly doing it for themselves." It states a fact about how people behave.
KEYWORDS: OUGHT claim, normative, own interests, single overriding concern
Ethical Egoism
Focus: what people SHOULD do
Lecture: "We should make our own ends, our own interests, as the single overriding concern."
Scenario: "You should always put your own interest first." It states what people should do.
!
Counterexamples the lecture offers against pure self-interest

Generosity (a child sharing ice cream with a dog), Charity (giving food to needy people), and Hospitality (a Papua New Guinea community welcoming a visitor): things done toward the other.

The Myth of Gyges and Socrates' Answer

KEYWORDS: ring, invisible, anonymous, shepherd, Glaucon, Plato's Republic, not being caught
Myth of Gyges (Ring of Gyges)
Focus: would you still be just if you could never be caught?
Lecture: Plato's Republic (Book II). Glaucon tells the story of Gyges the shepherd, who is given a ring that makes him invisible and anonymous. He seduces a queen, kills the king, and takes over the kingdom. Plato argues that the ring (invisibility and anonymity) is the only barrier between a just and an unjust person. Glaucon's challenge: what we really care for is our self-interest rather than justice or moral goodness. One of the reading quotes says those who practice justice do so involuntarily, because they lack the power to be unjust.
Scenario: Would you still return a found wallet if you were 100% sure nobody would ever know?
KEYWORDS: harmony, reason on top, will, appetite, justice to oneself
Socrates: Harmony of the Soul
Focus: the REASON for being ethical
Lecture: The good human life stems from a proper harmony of the parts of the soul. Reason, the "highest" part, is in charge, followed by the "lower" parts: will and appetite. Proper order = justice. Reversed order (appetites on top, reason at the base, desires running rampant) = injustice. Does it make sense to be ethical? Yes, because to be ethical means doing justice to oneself.
Scenario: You return the extra change a cashier gave you even though nobody noticed, because keeping your own inner order matters more than the extra money.

Look-alikes to avoid mixing up

Relativism vs Subjectivism
Relativism: the standard is the culture. Subjectivism: the standard is the individual.
Psych vs Ethical Egoism
Psychological = what people do (IS). Ethical = what people ought to do (OUGHT).
Normative System vs Social Norms
Normative system tells people how they ought to behave (rules, values, principles). Social norms are standards or rules that guide behavior within a society or group.
Morality vs Moral Judgment
Morality is the principles and standards. Moral judgment is the process of deciding whether an action is right or wrong.
Morality vs Law
Law tells what to avoid. What is legal is not necessarily moral.

Module 3: Ethical Levels and Conscience

Focus: three levels of ethics, then the internal judge (conscience) and its six kinds, including doubtful, scrupulous, lax, and erroneous.

The Three Ethical Levels

LEVEL 1: INDIVIDUAL
Personal Level

Can the person tell good from evil, and do they feel obligated and accountable for their own actions?

LEVEL 2: COMMUNITY
Societal Level

Ethical concerns that affect society at large: corruption, poverty, violence, and similar issues.

LEVEL 3: ECOSYSTEM
Environmental Level

Ethical concerns about humanity's impact on the natural world: pollution, deforestation, and similar issues.

Personal Level

KEYWORDS: distinguishes good from evil, feels obligated, feels accountable
The Ethos of Man: 3 traits
Focus: the person's own moral sense
Lecture: (1) He/she is able to distinguish between good and evil, right and wrong, moral and immoral. (2) He/she feels within himself/herself an obligation to do what is good and to avoid what is evil. (3) He/she feels accountable for his/her actions, expecting reward or punishment for them.
Scenario: You know on your own that copying answers is wrong, you feel you should not do it, and you expect consequences if you do.
KEYWORDS: endowed by nature, moral sense, dignity, dictates of reason
Moral sense and "dictates of reason" (Agapay)
Focus: conduct guided by reason
Lecture: "Man is endowed by nature with a moral sense. He/She is self-conscious of his dignity and submits to the duty of doing what is good and avoiding what is wrong. This much is expected of man: that he/she conducts himself/herself according to the dictates of reason."

Sanctions and Penalties (external)

#The four sanctions in the lecture
1The penal laws of our country
2Death as punishment for sin (Bible, Old Testament)
3Physical and mental diseases
4Human suffering

Conscience (internal)

KEYWORDS: Latin conscientia, trial of oneself, accusation and defense, practical judgment of reason
Conscience: two definitions
Focus: the internal judge
Lecture: Tillich: from the Latin "conscientia," meaning "trial of oneself," both in accusation and in defense. Panizo: an act of the practical judgment of reason deciding upon an individual action as good and to be performed, or as evil and to be avoided.
Scenario: Feeling guilty about something you did, even though nobody caught you.

The Six Kinds of Conscience

KEYWORDS: judges good as good, evil as evil
1. Correct / True Conscience
Focus: gets the call RIGHT
Lecture: It judges what is good as good and what is evil as evil.
Lecture example: It is correct when it tells that getting the property of another without consent is stealing.
Scenario: You know that taking someone's phone without asking is stealing.
KEYWORDS: judges good as evil and evil as good, mistaken
2. Erroneous / False Conscience
Focus: gets the call BACKWARDS
Lecture: Judges incorrectly that what is good is evil and what is evil is good.
Lecture example: Erroneous conscience is what tells the husband to have a mistress.
Scenario: A student believes that helping a friend cheat is "loyal and good."
KEYWORDS: subjective assurance, lawfulness or unlawfulness, sure of decision
3. Certain Conscience
Focus: fully SURE
Lecture: A subjective assurance of the lawfulness or unlawfulness of a certain act. The person is sure of his/her decision.
Scenario: You are completely sure cheating on an exam is wrong, with no hesitation.
KEYWORDS: unable to form a definite judgment, settle doubts first
4. Doubtful Conscience
Focus: DOUBTING, not yet decided
Lecture: Unable to form a definite judgment on a certain action. It must first be allowed to settle its doubts before an action is performed.
Scenario: You find money on the street and pause, unsure whether it is okay to keep it.
KEYWORDS: rigorous, extremely afraid of committing evil, meticulous, incontrovertible proofs
5. Scrupulous Conscience
Focus: TOO afraid of evil
Lecture: Rigorous conscience, extremely afraid of committing evil. It is meticulous and wants incontrovertible proofs before it acts.
Scenario: Someone agonizes for days over whether an innocent joke was secretly a lie and keeps asking for proof it was fine.
KEYWORDS: refuses to be bothered, rushes on, quick to justify itself
6. Lax Conscience
Focus: TOO relaxed about good and evil
Lecture: Refuses to be bothered about the distinction of good and evil. It rushes on and is quick to justify itself.
Scenario: Someone shrugs off cheating with "everyone does it anyway."

Sanction vs Conscience
Sanction is external (laws, suffering). Conscience is internal (your own judgment).
Erroneous vs Doubtful

Scrupulous vs Lax

CHAPTER 04
Module 4: The Moral Agent

Focus: who can be held accountable, what shapes a person, and the reasons behind moral choices (Kohlberg).

Moral Person, Moral Patient, Moral Agent

KEYWORDS: capacity, moral decision, perception of right and wrong, conscious of right and wrong
Moral Agency and Moral Agent
Focus: who can be held responsible
Lecture: Moral agency means "the capacity to make moral decision based on the perception of right and wrong." A moral agent is a "being who is conscious of the concepts of right and wrong." There is no moral society without its agents: moral agents are the implementors of moral concepts. Only human adults, or humans already capable of making informed decisions, can be moral agents. Only a moral agent can be responsible and accountable.
Lecture example: Students, teachers, and school administrators act as moral agents. Example given: practicing honesty by not copying.
Scenario: A student who submits original work and does not copy during exams is acting as a moral agent.
KEYWORDS: recipient, receiver of moral actions, rights but no duties
Moral Patient
Focus: can be helped or harmed, but not a source of moral action
Lecture: All moral persons are moral patients (recipients). All human persons can be receivers of morally evaluable actions, but only some can be sources of them. Normal human adults and infants, being moral persons, are moral patients. All moral persons have moral rights, but not all have moral duties.
Scenario: An infant can be harmed or cared for, but cannot be held responsible for actions.
✓
Quick rule

Moral patient = rights only. Moral agent = rights AND duties (so accountable).

The Four Crosspoints (Dr. Ramon C. Reyes)

Identity, "who one is or who I am," is a product of many forces and events that happened outside of one's choosing.

CROSSPOINT 1
Physical

Physical events or material factors such as physical attributes, genes, or nationality. Example: place of birth and socioeconomic status; a child born into poverty faces different ethical struggles (education, healthcare) than one born into wealth.

CROSSPOINT 2
Interpersonal

How parents, relatives, peers, classmates, or workmates made an impact on your personality. Example: a child raised in a strict religious household may develop different ethical views than one raised in a more secular home.

CROSSPOINT 3
Societal

The impact of society, including cultural beliefs, customs, and practices you did not choose. Example: living under strict laws on personal freedoms and facing dilemmas about following or resisting them.

CROSSPOINT 4
Historical

Your history shapes who you are now; your past is beyond your control. Example: a young soldier drafted into a war he did not start must decide whether to follow orders or question war crimes.

The lecture says the point is the reasoning behind a choice, not whether someone says "steal" or "don't steal."

Postconventional5. Social Contract and Individual RightsAsk "what makes for a good society?"; laws should be based on justice and can be changed if unfair.A citizen protests an unfair law because it violates human rights.
Postconventional6. Universal (Ethical) PrinciplesJustice means treating all impartially and respecting everyone's dignity, even against laws; self-chosen principles.A person hides a refugee in their home, though illegal, believing in the moral duty to protect human life.

The Heinz Dilemma

Heinz's wife is dying. A druggist charges $2,000 for a drug that cost $200 to make. Heinz can raise only about $1,000. The druggist refuses to lower the price or accept later payment, so Heinz breaks in and steals it. Should he have? There is no single correct answer: the reasoning is what is scored.

StageHeinz should steal because...Heinz should NOT steal because...
1. ObedienceIt is only worth $200; he offered to pay and was not stealing anything else.He will be put in prison, which will mean he is a bad person.
2. Self-interestHe will be much happier if he saves his wife, even with a prison sentence.Prison is an awful place; he would more likely languish in a cell than mourn his wife.
3. ConformityHis wife expects it; he wants to be a good husband.Stealing is bad and he is not a criminal; he tried everything without breaking the law.
4. Law and orderHe should take the prescribed punishment and pay the druggist what he is owed.Criminals cannot run around without regard for the law; actions have consequences.
5. Social contractEveryone has a right to choose life, regardless of the law.The scientist has a right to fair compensation; his wife's sickness does not make it right.
6. Universal ethicsSaving a human life is a more fundamental value than another person's property rights.Others may need the medicine just as badly, and their lives are equally significant.

Rights, Duties, and Moral Agency

KEYWORDS: bearer of rights, higher than legal rights, identical to human rights
Moral Rights
Focus: rights every person has
Lecture: "To be a person is to be a bearer of rights." Moral rights are the rights of humans that are over and above, or higher than, their legal rights. Moral rights are identical to "human rights."
Scenario: A person has the right to life, whether or not a particular country's law states it.
KEYWORDS: rights correlate duties, only moral agents have both, responsible and accountable
Rights and Duties
Focus: why only agents are accountable
Lecture: Rights correlate duties. To protect and promote another person's rights, there is a need for duties and responsibilities. Every moral person has rights, but not all moral persons can do their duties. Only moral agents have both rights and duties, so only a moral agent can be responsible and accountable for his/her actions.
Scenario: Your classmates' right to fair grades is protected because everyone has the duty not to cheat.

Reason, Freedom, and Responsibility

KEYWORDS: accountability, rationality = reason (intelligence) + free will (freedom)
Rationality, Reason and Freedom
Focus: the source of accountability
Lecture: Accountability is a natural product of our rationality, which consists of our reason (or intelligence) and free will (or freedom).
Scenario: You work out that lying is wrong (reason), then choose not to lie (freedom), so you own the result (responsibility).
KEYWORDS: power of the soul, in control of one's actions, act otherwise or not act at all
The Will (Ocampo)
Focus: real control over your actions
Lecture: The will is the power of the soul that starts the dynamics of what it takes human beings to act. It is that power by which a human being is in control of his actions. To have control means that as a person acts, it is possible to act otherwise or not to act at all.
Scenario: You could easily cheat and get away with it, but you choose not to.
KEYWORDS: human limitation, also power, implications in morality
"Sapagkat ako'y tao lamang"
Focus: being human is a limit AND a power
Lecture: Being "tao" (only human) points out human limitations, but being human also possesses the power. It has implications in the realm of morality.

Look-alikes to avoid mixing up

Agent vs Patient
Agent: rights and duties, accountable. Patient: rights only, can be helped or harmed.
Stage 3 vs 4
Stage 3 pleases the people close to you. Stage 4 obeys the law for social order.
Stage 4 vs 5
Stage 4: the law is the law. Stage 5: laws must be just and can be changed.
Stage 5 vs 6
Stage 5 works within a social contract. Stage 6 follows self-chosen universal principles even against laws.
Stage 1 vs 2
Stage 1 avoids punishment. Stage 2 seeks personal reward.
