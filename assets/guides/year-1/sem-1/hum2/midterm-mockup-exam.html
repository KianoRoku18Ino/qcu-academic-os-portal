CHAPTER 01 · PART I
Multiple Choice

Choose the best answer. Items 1–20.

ITEM 1 · M1
"Ethos," the Greek root of the word ethics, literally means:
Answer: Characteristic way of acting. Ethos = characteristic way of acting. Its Latin equivalent mos/mores means tradition or custom.
ITEM 2 · M1
A company surveys employees on whether they feel pressure to compromise principles. This is which area of ethics?
Answer: Descriptive ethics. Descriptive ethics is empirical research into actual attitudes and beliefs.
ITEM 3 · M1
"What is goodness, and how can we tell good from bad?" is a question of:
Answer: Metaethics. Metaethics studies the nature of ethical properties, judgments and attitudes.
ITEM 4 · M1
"It is wrong to wear a leather vest over a Barong Tagalog" is an example of which valuation?
Answer: Aesthetics. It is a matter of taste and personal approval, not moral right or wrong.
ITEM 5 · M1
A dilemma is best defined as:
Answer: A difficult choice between alternatives, especially equally undesirable ones. Issue = public debate; judgment = assessment; decision = choice; dilemma = hard choice between alternatives.
ITEM 6 · M2
Which statement correctly contrasts law with morality?
Answer: Law only tells what to avoid; what is legal is not necessarily moral. Morality is about what to do; law mostly tells what to avoid.
ITEM 7 · M2
The strongest criticism of cultural relativism is that it:
Answer: Prevents us from criticizing other cultures' practices. If no outside standard exists, even harmful practices such as FGM cannot be called wrong.
ITEM 8 · M2
Counter to "No one knows my situation better than myself":
Answer: Many human experiences are common, and others may have something useful to suggest. Subjectivism claim 2 is countered by the commonality of human experience.
ITEM 9 · M2
Psychological egoism holds that:
Answer: All human actions are ultimately motivated by self-interest. Psychological egoism is descriptive (what people do), unlike ethical egoism.
ITEM 10 · M2
Socrates' answer to why one should be ethical (Republic, Book IV):
Answer: Justice is the harmony of the soul with reason in charge. Reason rules will and appetite; that internal order is justice, valuable in itself.
ITEM 11 · M3
Which is NOT one of the six kinds of conscience?
Answer: Rational. The six: correct/true, erroneous/false, certain, doubtful, scrupulous, lax.
ITEM 12 · M3
A doubtful conscience:
Answer: Cannot form a definite judgment and must settle its doubts before acting. Doubtful = unresolved; erroneous = decided wrongly.
ITEM 13 · M3
A conscience that is meticulous, extremely afraid of evil, and demands incontrovertible proof is:
Answer: Scrupulous. Scrupulous overreacts to possible evil; lax barely reacts.
ITEM 14 · M3
Which is an external accountability mechanism at the Personal Level?
Answer: Sanctions such as penal laws. Sanctions are imposed from outside; conscience is internal.
ITEM 15 · M4
An infant is best described as a:
Answer: Moral patient. Infants have moral rights but cannot be held responsible; they have no duties.
ITEM 16 · M4
A young man drafted into a war he did not start illustrates which crosspoint?
Answer: Historical. Historical crosspoint: past events and conflicts beyond one's control.
ITEM 17 · M4
Doing one's duty and obeying laws to maintain social order is Kohlberg's:
Answer: Stage 4. Stage 4 = Law and Order / Maintaining the Social Order (Conventional).
ITEM 18 · M4
The Heinz Dilemma is used to measure:
Answer: The stage of moral reasoning behind a person's answer. Kohlberg scores the reasoning, not whether one says steal or not.
ITEM 19 · M5
The Principle of Utility states that the most moral action is the one that:
Answer: Produces the greatest happiness for the greatest number. Right action is judged by usefulness in producing pleasure/happiness.
ITEM 20 · M5
Which criterion of the Hedonistic Calculus asks "How soon will it occur?"
Answer: Propinquity. Propinquity = nearness in time.
CHAPTER 02 · PART II
True or False

Items 21–30.

ITEM 21 · M1
Ethics and legality are always the same.
Answer: False. Something can be legal but morally wrong.
ITEM 22 · M2
Cultural relativism affirms that universal truths in ethics exist.
Answer: False. It says there are only cultural codes.
ITEM 23 · M2
Ethical egoism is a descriptive claim about how people actually behave.
Answer: False. It is normative (ought); psychological egoism is descriptive.
ITEM 24 · M3
A lax conscience is quick to justify itself.
Answer: True. Lax conscience refuses to be bothered about good versus evil and rushes on.
ITEM 25 · M4
Only moral agents have both rights and duties.
Answer: True. Moral patients have rights but not duties.
ITEM 26 · M4
Accountability is a natural product of reason and free will.
Answer: True. Rationality = reason + free will.
ITEM 27 · M5
Bentham wanted ethics to be quantitative, like Newton's science.
Answer: True. He sought to make happiness and the good measurable.
ITEM 28 · M5
Hedons are standard units of pain.
Answer: False. Hedons = pleasure; dolors = pain.
ITEM 29 · M1
Descriptive ethics sets the criteria for what is morally right and wrong.
Answer: False. That is normative ethics; descriptive ethics reports actual beliefs.
ITEM 30 · M5
Utilitarianism judges an action mainly by the intention behind it.
Answer: False. It judges by consequences.

CHAPTER 04 · PART IV
Application Items

Scenario-based items. Items 41–50.

ITEM 41 · M4
A teenager follows school rules mainly to be seen as "good" by teachers and friends. Which stage?
Answer: Stage 3. Approval-seeking conformity = Good Interpersonal Relationships.
ITEM 42 · M4
An employee will not steal because it is against the law, even if no one would find out. Which stage?
Answer: Stage 4. Duty to society and obedience to law.
ITEM 43 · M1
Two students return a lost wallet: one because it is right, one from fear of punishment. Best analysis:
Answer: Not equally moral, because their motivations differ. Same act, different motivation: principle versus Stage 1 fear. (Consequence-only views see them as equal.)
ITEM 44 · M2
A student helps a classmate because it also feels good. Psychological egoism, if true, would show:
Answer: Nothing about whether people ought to act selfishly. An "is" claim does not prove an "ought" claim; feeling good can be a side effect.
ITEM 45 · M2
A community practice causes serious harm, but a relativist says no one outside may judge it. This exposes which limitation?
Answer: Cultural relativism cannot criticize other cultures. It leaves no standard to call even harmful practices wrong.
ITEM 46 · M5
Using the lesson's example, Rural life scores 22 and Urban life 19. What does the calculus recommend?
Answer: Rural life, with a net utility 3 points higher. Choose the greater overall utility: 22 versus 19.
ITEM 47 · M5
A doctor kills one healthy janitor to save five patients. The lesson uses this to argue that utilitarianism:
Answer: Can justify violating human rights, so it may be false. Reductio: if utilitarianism is true the act was right; it was not; so the theory fails.
ITEM 48 · M4
A child born into poverty rather than wealth illustrates which crosspoint?
Answer: Physical. Place of birth and living conditions belong to the Physical crosspoint.
ITEM 49 · M3
A conscience that tells a husband it is fine to have a mistress is:
Answer: Erroneous. It judges evil as good (lesson's own example).
ITEM 50 · M5
Cushman's story (Jon misses a killing; Matt accidentally kills while meaning to scare) mainly raises the problem of:
Answer: Intention versus consequence in judging responsibility. Utilitarianism stresses outcomes, yet we often care about intent.
