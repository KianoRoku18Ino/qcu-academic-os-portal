Lesson Overview

Week 4-5 answers the question CC101 has been building toward: what is actually inside "a computer"? It breaks a computer down into its three basic components — Hardware, Software, and Peopleware — then goes one level deeper into each: Hardware splits into Input/Output/Storage devices and gets a dedicated tour of what's physically on the motherboard; Software splits into System Software (what runs the machine) and Application Software (what you actually use); and Peopleware closes the loop by naming the human side of the system, including the three practical categories of computer user.

🔗
Where This Fits in Your Roadmap

This is foundational hardware/software vocabulary that gets assumed background once you reach dedicated hardware or OS-level courses later in the program (e.g. a computer architecture or operating systems course). CC101 itself stays Layer 0 — no prerequisites, gateway course.

CHAPTER 01
The Computer System

Everything else this week is a deeper look inside one of these three boxes. Get this split first — it's the skeleton the rest of the lesson hangs on.

DEFINITION
Computer System
A complete, working computer. It includes not just the computer itself, but also any software and peripheral devices necessary to make it function.

The Three Basic Components

PHYSICAL
Hardware

Anything physically connected to the computer, or something you can physically touch. Splits into Input, Output, and Storage Devices.

INSTRUCTIONS
Software

The set of instructions/programs that tell the computer what to do. Splits into System Software and Application Software.

HUMAN
Peopleware

The human role inside an IT system — the people who use, plan, and manage the technology.

🎤
Recitation-Ready Answer

If asked "what are the components of a computer system?" — say it like this: "A computer system is a complete working computer — that's hardware, software, and peopleware together. Hardware is the physical parts, software is the instructions that run on the hardware, and peopleware is the people who actually use and manage the system. Take any one of the three away and you don't have a working computer system anymore."

💡
Comparison: Computer vs. Computer System

The closest related — and most confusable — term is just "computer" on its own. A computer is the physical box/device; a computer system is that device plus the software and peripherals needed to actually make it functional. A brand-new PC with no operating system installed and no keyboard/monitor attached is a computer, but not yet a working computer system.

📌
Concrete Example

A QCU computer-lab PC counts as a computer system: the case and its internal parts (hardware), Windows plus your typing/browser software (software), and the lab technician or the student using it (peopleware) — all three together, not any one piece alone.

🎤 Recitation Drill — Chapter 01
Q1
"What is a computer system, and what are its three basic components?"
A complete, working computer — including the software and peripherals needed to function. Its three components are Hardware (physical parts), Software (the instructions), and Peopleware (the human role).
Q2
"How is a computer system different from just 'a computer'?"
"Computer" usually refers to just the physical device. "Computer system" is broader — it includes the software and peripheral devices that make that device actually usable.
CHAPTER 02
Hardware & Storage Devices

Hardware is the physical layer — anything you can touch. The lesson splits it into three categories by what direction the data moves.

DEFINITION
Hardware
Best described as a device that is physically connected to your computer, or something that can be physically touched.

The Three Categories

DATA IN
Input Devices

Any device that lets a person communicate information to the computer, so it can perform its tasks.
Examples: Digital Camera, Digitizing Tablet, Keyboard, Microphone, Mouse.

DATA OUT
Output Devices

A device that receives data from another device and generates output with it, but cannot send data back to another device.
Examples: Monitor, Printer, Speaker, Projector.

DATA HELD
Storage Devices

Hardware used for storing, porting, and extracting data files and objects — can hold data both temporarily and permanently, and can be internal or external.

🎤
Recitation-Ready Answer — Input vs. Output

"Input devices bring information into the computer — think keyboard, mouse, microphone. Output devices take information the computer already processed and present it back to you — think monitor, printer, speaker. The direction of data flow is the whole difference: in vs. out."

Primary vs. Secondary Storage

Storage Devices split into two types — and this is exactly the spot flagged in the Verification Notes above, so the correction is repeated here where it matters most for recitation.

AS STATED ON SLIDE
Primary Storage Devices
Also called main memory; the slide labels it "a non-volatile, temporary type of storage." Examples: RAM, ROM, PROM, EEPROM.
AS STATED ON SLIDE
Secondary Storage Devices
Also called auxiliary memory; the slide labels it "a volatile, permanent type of storage." Examples: Hard Disk Drive, Floppy Disk Drive, Optical Disk, Flash Drive, Memory Card.
⚠️
Corrected — say it this way in recitation

The slide's "volatile/non-volatile" labels are swapped. The technically accurate statement is: Primary storage (RAM specifically) is volatile and temporary — it loses its contents the instant power is cut, which is exactly why it's called "temporary." Secondary storage is non-volatile and permanent — a flash drive or hard disk keeps its data with no power at all, which is the entire reason it's used for long-term storage. (ROM, PROM, and EEPROM, also listed under Primary, are actually non-volatile themselves — they're grouped under "Primary/main memory" because of where they sit in the system, not because they share RAM's volatility.)

💡
Comparison: RAM vs. ROM (the pair that trips people up)

RAM (Random Access Memory) is volatile, temporary, and read-and-write — it's your computer's short-term working space. ROM (Read-Only Memory) is non-volatile and, as the name says, generally read-only — it holds instructions (like startup firmware) that need to survive a power-off. Same "Primary Storage" category, opposite behavior.

🎤 Recitation Drill — Chapter 02
Q1
"Give one example each of an input, output, and storage device."
Input: keyboard. Output: monitor. Storage: flash drive (secondary) or RAM (primary).
Q2
"What's the difference between primary and secondary storage, in terms of volatility?"
Primary storage (RAM) is volatile and temporary — it clears when power is lost. Secondary storage (hard disks, flash drives) is non-volatile and permanent — it keeps data with the power off.
Q3
"Name the four examples of Primary Storage Devices."
RAM, ROM, PROM, and EEPROM.
CHAPTER 03
Inside the Motherboard

The lesson's "How Does a Computer Work" segment opens the case and names every major part sitting on or plugging into the motherboard. This is the single densest, most named-part-heavy stretch of the week.

DEFINITION
Motherboard
One of the most essential parts of a computer system. It holds together many of the crucial components of a computer, including the CPU, memory, and connectors for input and output devices.

Named Components

COMPONENT
Socket
A connection that allows a computer processor to be connected to a motherboard. Example on slide: Socket 370 / PGA370.
COMPONENT
ATX Power Connector
Connectors inside your computer that connect the computer's power supply to an ATX-style motherboard. The slide shows both an older-style connector cable and the 24-pin ATX power supply connector.
COMPONENT
Memory Slot
Also called a memory socket or RAM slot — what allows computer memory (RAM) to be inserted into the computer. A motherboard may have 2 to 4 memory slots (sometimes more on high-end boards); the slots determine which type of RAM the board can use.
COMPONENT
DIMM
Dual In-line Memory Module — a circuit board that holds memory chips. DIMMs have a 64-bit data path because of Pentium-processor requirements, which means they can be installed one at a time — unlike the older SIMMs on a Pentium, which had to be added in pairs.

Types of RAM

  • DRAM — Dynamic Random Access Memory
  • SRAM — Static Random Access Memory
  • DDR — Double Data Rate, used for desktop computers; SODIMM (Small Outline Dual Inline Memory Module) is the laptop-computer equivalent
  • SDRAM — Synchronous Dynamic Random Access Memory

Storage & Expansion Interfaces

COMPONENT
IDE
Integrated Drive Electronics — a standard interface for connecting a motherboard to storage devices such as hard drives and CD-ROM/DVD drives. The original IDE had a 16-bit interface connecting two devices to a single ribbon cable.
COMPONENT
Northbridge
An integrated circuit responsible for communications between the CPU interface, AGP, and memory — directly connected to those components and acting like a "bridge" so the Southbridge chip can reach the CPU, RAM, and graphics controller.
COMPONENT
Southbridge
An integrated circuit on the motherboard responsible for the hard-drive controller, I/O controller, and integrated hardware such as the sound card and video card (if present), plus USB, PCI, ISA IDE, BIOS, and Ethernet.
COMPONENT
AGP
Accelerated Graphics Port — an advanced port designed for video cards and 3D accelerators. Introduces a dedicated point-to-point channel that lets the graphics controller access system memory directly.
COMPONENT
PCI
Peripheral Component Interconnect — used to add peripherals to your computer, such as a graphics card, modem, or additional USB ports.
⚠️
IDE's alternate name — say "Integrated," not "intelligent"

The slide lists "intelligent drive electronics" as an alternate expansion of IDE alongside ATA. If your professor asks you to spell out the acronym, lead with Integrated Drive Electronics — that's the expansion used by virtually every standards body and technical reference. "Intelligent" shows up in a few informal glossaries but isn't the one to give first.

🌐
Context Not on the Slide — Verified via Web Search

The slides don't date IDE's introduction. For extra context (not testable from this lecture, but useful background): IDE/ATA was introduced in 1986 by Western Digital and Compaq, and was formally standardized by ANSI as "AT Attachment" (ATA) in November 1990. Source: TechTarget's IDE/ATA glossary entry.

🎤 Recitation Drill — Chapter 03
Q1
"What connects the CPU to the motherboard?"
The Socket — a connection that allows a computer processor to be attached to the motherboard.
Q2
"What's the difference between Northbridge and Southbridge?"
Northbridge handles communication between the CPU, AGP, and memory — the "faster," more performance-critical connections. Southbridge handles the hard-drive controller, I/O controller, USB, PCI, BIOS, and Ethernet — generally the slower peripheral-facing side, and it talks to the CPU/RAM/graphics through the Northbridge.
Q3
"Why can DIMMs be installed one at a time, unlike older SIMMs?"
Because DIMMs have a full 64-bit data path (matching what the Pentium processor requires on its own), so a single DIMM already satisfies the bus width. Older SIMMs were narrower, so a Pentium-era board needed two installed together to reach the same 64-bit width.
CHAPTER 04
Software

If Hardware is what you can touch, Software is what tells it what to do. The lesson splits Software into two categories by who it's for: the machine itself, or the end user.

DEFINITION
Software
In its most general sense, a set of instructions or programs instructing a computer to do specific tasks — a generic term used to describe computer programs.

1. System Software

Consists of programs designed to facilitate the use of the computer by the user — software required to support the production or execution of application programs, but which is not specific to any particular application.

SYSTEM SOFTWARE
Operating System

The most important software that runs on a computer. Manages the computer's memory and processes, as well as all of its software and hardware. Most common: Microsoft Windows, MacOS, Linux.

SYSTEM SOFTWARE
Device Drivers

A program that controls a particular type of device attached to your computer — there are device drivers for printers, displays, and so on.

SYSTEM SOFTWARE
Utility Programs

A type of system software that lets a user perform maintenance-type tasks, usually related to managing the computer, its devices, or its programs. Examples: File Manager, Disk Defragmenter.

2. Application Software

A term used for software created for a specific purpose — generally a program or collection of programs used by end users.

TypeExamples (per slide)
Word processing softwareMS Word, WordPad, Notepad
Database softwareOracle, MS Access
Spreadsheet softwareApple Numbers, Microsoft Excel
Multimedia softwareReal Player, Media Player
Presentation softwareMicrosoft PowerPoint, Keynote
Enterprise softwareCustomer relationship management (CRM) system
APPLICATION TYPE
Word Processing
Creating and editing documents on a computer, mimicking the format/style of a typewriter. Requires a computer and word-processing software; a printer can create a physical copy.
APPLICATION TYPE
Database Software
Used for creating, editing, and maintaining database files/records — storing data as structured fields, tables, and columns retrievable directly or programmatically. Also called database management software (DBMS), though the terms aren't exact synonyms.
APPLICATION TYPE
Spreadsheet Software
Organizes, stores, and analyzes data in tabular form — a digital simulation of paper accounting worksheets, with multiple interacting sheets and text/numeric/graphic data.
APPLICATION TYPE
Multimedia Software
Capable of playing or recording audio files, or playing or recording video files.
APPLICATION TYPE
Presentation Software
Designed to let users create a presentation of ideas by stringing together text, images, and audio/video — tells a story or supports a speech.
APPLICATION TYPE
Enterprise Software
An over-arching term for any software used in large organizations (business or government) — an essential part of a computer-based information system, providing tools like online payment processing and automated billing.
💡
Comparison: System vs. Application Software

"System software keeps the computer itself running — the OS, drivers, utilities. Application software is what a person actually opens to get work done — Word, Excel, PowerPoint. System software supports every application; application software is what you, the end user, actually see and use."

🎤 Recitation Drill — Chapter 04
Q1
"Give the two categories of computer software and one example of each."
System Software — e.g., an Operating System like Windows. Application Software — e.g., word processing software like MS Word.
Q2
"What does an operating system manage?"
The computer's memory and processes, as well as all of its software and hardware.
Q3
"Name three of the six application software types on the slide."
Any three of: word processing, database, spreadsheet, multimedia, presentation, enterprise.
CHAPTER 05
Peopleware & Types of Users

The last piece of the Hardware/Software/Peopleware split — the human side of a computer system. The slide covering this topic is presented twice, back to back, with identical wording; both copies are treated as one topic here.

DEFINITION
Peopleware
Refers to the human role in an IT system. The term treats human talent as a kind of commodified piece of an IT process — a key part of providing various technical business models and other planning resources.

Types of Computer User

TYPE 1
Casual / Regular User

Uses a computer for everyday, general-purpose tasks — the baseline user category on the slide.

TYPE 2
Small Office/Home Office (SOHO)

A user working out of a small office or home-office setup — a category between casual home use and full enterprise/business use.

TYPE 3
Power User

A more advanced user with heavier computing needs than a casual user — typically pushing hardware/software further than default use cases.

💡
Comparison: Peopleware vs. Users

"Peopleware" is the broader concept — the human role in the system generally (this covers IT staff, planners, and technical talent, not just end users). "Types of computer user" is a narrower classification specifically about how a person uses the machine day to day. Every "type of user" is an instance of peopleware, but peopleware also includes people who never personally sit at the keyboard (e.g., the planners the slide references).

🎤 Recitation Drill — Chapter 05
Q1
"What does 'peopleware' refer to?"
The human role in an IT system — human talent as part of the IT process and technical business planning.
Q2
"Name the three types of computer user from the lecture."
Casual/regular user, Small Office/Home Office (SOHO), and Power User.
