CHAPTER 00
Course Overview & Study Priority Map

This guide consolidates two official lecture presentations issued by the NCR Regional Community Defense Group, Army Reserve Command (Fort Andres Bonifacio, Metro Manila): Standards of Conduct of a Soldier and AFP Customs and Traditions. Together they form the baseline conduct doctrine every ROTC cadet is expected to internalize before moving into leadership and drill-focused modules — the six standards of conduct define who a soldier is expected to be, while the traditions and customs define how that identity is expressed in practice, from ceremony to everyday courtesy.

🔗
Critical Path Context

These two topics are taught back-to-back because they share one throughline: internalized values (Ch. 01) are what make customs and traditions (Ch. 02–05) meaningful rather than empty ritual. Expect recitation or exam items to test exact recall — the six standards, the five traditions, and specific named customs — more than open-ended analysis, so treat the named lists as your first memorization target.

CHAPTER 01
Standards of Conduct

The lesson scope groups this topic into three parts: Core Values, Standards of Conduct, and Applications. In practice, the six items below are themselves called the "Standards of Conduct" — they operationalize a soldier's core values into observable behavior expected in training, combat, peacekeeping, and the implementation of national policy.

The Six Standards of Conduct

01 · MISSION FOCUS
Pursuit of Excellence
Each soldier strives to perfect his craft to achieve his mission — full knowledge of his job, proficiency in training and combat, and competence in peacekeeping and the implementation of national development policies.
02 · IDENTITY
Pride in Uniform
The uniform reflects both the individual soldier and the whole organization. Careful maintenance of the uniform and its insignia is what visibly sets a soldier apart from the rest.
03 · SELF-CONTROL
Discipline
A soldier is a model of self-discipline. It is vital to both preservation of life and mission success, and demands unconditional compliance as its foremost aspect — enforced by authority and followed without reservation.
04 · COMPETENCE
Professionalism
Commitment, knowledge, and skill expressed through attention to duty, discipline, and competence — giving rise to the impartiality, objectivity, and non-partisanship needed to serve the population's welfare.
05 · LEGALITY
Adherence to Law
Soldiers are both citizens and military personnel, subject to the same law and duty of service. Where civil and military standards conflict, the higher standard required under either system always prevails.
06 · HIERARCHY
Deference to Authority
Authority flows through the Chain of Command — from the Non-Commissioned Officer as implementer, through each Commanding Officer in succession. Deference to civil authority is likewise part of a soldier's responsibility.

Application

Responsibility for instilling and reinforcing these values rests with military leadership — officers and NCOs in command are expected to have them committed to memory and be able to clarify them on demand.

A soldier applies these standards by drawing on knowledge of human nature in situations training never explicitly covered. Values are taught through perception and practice over time, so steady self-review in daily life is what keeps the application of these standards accurate — and keeps the code itself relevant across decades.

✅
Key Takeaway

The six standards aren't a checklist to memorize once — they're meant to be re-applied continuously through self-review, which is exactly why "Application" is treated as its own lesson section rather than a footnote.

CHAPTER 02
AFP Traditions
DEFINITION
Traditions
AFP Customs and Traditions lecture
Inherited principles, standards, and practices that serve as established guides for an individual or group.

The Five Military Traditions

Tradition of Valor
COMBAT LEGACY
Bataan & Corregidor
Rooted in the gallant defense of Bataan and Corregidor against the Japanese Imperial Army during World War II.
Tradition of Duty
PUBLIC SERVICE
Responsibility & Self-Sacrifice
Being a dedicated public servant — carrying a deep sense of responsibility and a readiness for self-sacrifice.
Tradition of Honor
CHARACTER
Dignity & Supreme Sacrifice
Personal dignity and self-worth, extending to supreme sacrifice in the cause of winning freedom.
Tradition of Solidarity
TEAMWORK
Unity of Effort
Unity — working together through thick and thin, as part of the team and as a partner of the people in attaining national objectives.
Tradition of Loyalty
ALLEGIANCE
Six Objects of Loyalty
Loyalty shown to the nation, the constitution, the Commander-in-Chief, seniors, peers, and juniors.
PRIMARY SOURCE QUOTE
Gen. Douglas MacArthur, on the defense of Bataan and Corregidor
Cited under Tradition of Valor
"Give me 20,000 Filipino soldiers and I'll conquer the world." — offered in the lecture as the tribute that anchors this tradition's place in AFP identity.

CHAPTER 04
Courtesy Calls & Social Customs

Types of Courtesy Calls

A "courtesy call" is a formal visit an officer pays to a superior under specific circumstances. The lecture lists six recurring scenarios:

1
Rejoining a major service
An officer detailed outside of, and rejoining, his major service — or reassigned to units outside it — customarily calls on his Major Service Commander.
2
Returning from overseas duty
Upon arrival or departure, officers from an overseas mission or foreign schooling pay a call to their respective Major Service Commander.
3
Assuming or leaving a foreign post
Officers assuming a foreign post, or returning from one, call on the Chief of Staff and the Secretary of National Defense.
4
Assuming a command position
Officers assuming command or similar key positions call on their immediate commanders, including the Major Service Commander — and do the same prior to a transfer or relief from duty.
5
Newly promoted officers
Customarily call on their Major Service Commanders, usually done in groups of the same rank.
6
Newly promoted Generals / Flag Officers
Call on the Major Service Commanders, Chief of Staff AFP, and the Secretary of National Defense after taking their oath of office before the Commander-in-Chief; these calls are often accompanied by arrival and departure honors.
⚠️
Courtesy Note

Senior officers who are the subject of these calls may be too busy to receive every caller — but simply manifesting the intention to pay the call is itself considered a sign of courtesy and goodwill.

Officer Etiquette Essentials

🎩
Breeding & Gentlemanly Conduct

An officer's conduct and dealings are expected to show breeding:

  • Being late for any engagement is considered the height of bad manners.
  • He is expected to behave and conduct himself with dignity and restraint.
  • Addressing a woman by her first name or nickname is bad taste unless she has indicated otherwise.
  • An officer hosting a friend at his club stays present except when necessary to step away.
  • An officer stands when introduced to a woman, a clergyman, or an elderly man.
  • Complimenting one person in company is avoided, since others present might feel slighted.
CHAPTER 05
The Officer's Code

A closing set of five expectations that define professional integrity for a commissioned officer.

INTEGRITY
Official Statements

An officer's official statement — written or unwritten — must be made without reservation. A false official statement is contrary to military ethics and punishable under the Articles of War.

DUTY
Responsibilities

It is an officer's duty to prepare himself professionally for higher responsibilities — rank carries commensurate responsibility, and accepting a commission means preparing for what comes after it.

JUDGMENT
Tact and Foresight

An officer must be discreet in the exercise of his duties, and prepared to perform his tasks skillfully under all circumstances.

BEARING
Personal Appearance

An officer appears neat and clean at all times — nothing undermines subordinates' respect and confidence faster than a superior's shabbiness.

STEWARDSHIP
Welfare of Enlisted Men

The welfare of enlisted men and their families should be an officer's paramount concern.

🚫
Legal Consequence to Remember

"Official Statements" is the one item in this code with an explicit legal penalty attached — a false official statement isn't just an ethics lapse, it's punishable under the Articles of War. This is the most exam-testable line in this chapter.
