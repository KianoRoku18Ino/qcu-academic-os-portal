CHAPTER 00
Overview & Study Priority Map

This unit defines physical fitness as a state of health and well-being that lets you perform daily activities with energy, reduces disease risk, and supports an active lifestyle. It splits fitness into two families of components — health-related (why your body stays healthy) and skill-related (how well your body performs in sport/movement) — and ties inactivity directly to a set of preventable illnesses.

🔗
Critical Path Context

The 5 health-related and 6 skill-related components (Ch. 01–02) are the foundation for every fitness test PATHFIT1 will have you perform this semester — memorize the component-to-test pairing, not just the definitions, since practicals grade you on running the actual test correctly.

Physical Activity vs. Exercise

These terms are often used interchangeably but are tested as distinct concepts.

UNPLANNED · DAILY
Physical Activity
Any bodily movement produced by skeletal muscles that results in energy expenditure. Examples: walking, doing household chores, gardening, playing with friends, taking the stairs.
PLANNED · STRUCTURED
Exercise
Planned, structured, repetitive physical activity done to improve or maintain one or more components of fitness. Examples: running, weight training, swimming laps, cycling, circuit training.

Hypokinetic Diseases

⚠️
Diseases & health problems caused by lack of physical activity or prolonged inactivity

Obesity · Heart Disease · Type 2 Diabetes · Hypertension (High Blood Pressure) · Osteoporosis · Low Back Pain · Depression and Anxiety — remember the tagline: "Sit less, move more, live longer."

Why Inactivity Causes Disease

Heart & lungs
Inactivity weakens cardiovascular and respiratory efficiency over time.
Weight gain
Leads to unhealthy weight gain from unused energy expenditure.
Blood sugar
Affects blood sugar regulation and insulin function.
Bone strength
Reduces bone density and structural strength.
Mental health
Affects mental health and mood regulation.
💊
Regular Activity Is Medicine

This is the unit's core thesis — every test and habit taught afterward exists to counter this list.

CHAPTER 01
Health-Related Components of Physical Fitness

These 5 components are directly important for good health and everyday function — they answer "is your body healthy enough to do what you need it to do?" Each one has a standard field test attached; know both the definition and the test name.

HEALTH · 01
Cardiorespiratory Endurance

The ability of the heart, lungs, and blood vessels to deliver oxygen during sustained physical activity.
Test: 20-Meter Paced Run (Beep Test), 3-Minute Step Test.

HEALTH · 02
Muscular Strength

The amount of force a muscle or muscle group can exert in a single maximum effort.
Test: Push-Up Test (1RM or Max Reps), Curl-Up Test (1RM or Max Reps).

HEALTH · 03
Muscular Endurance

The ability of muscles to sustain repeated contractions over a period of time.
Test: Plank Hold Test (Max Time), Wall Sit Test (Max Time).

HEALTH · 04
Flexibility

The ability of a joint or series of joints to move through their full range of motion.
Test: Sit and Reach Test.

HEALTH · 05
Body Composition

The ratio of fat mass to lean mass in the body.
Test: Body Mass Index (BMI), Body Fat Percentage (Skinfold Caliper).

CHAPTER 02
Skill-Related Components of Physical Fitness

These 6 components improve performance in sports and physical activities — they answer "how well does your body move, react, and coordinate?" rather than baseline health.

SKILL · 01
Power

The ability to exert maximum force in the shortest time.
Test: Vertical Jump Test, Standing Broad Jump, Medicine Ball Throw (Chest Pass).

SKILL · 02
Speed

The ability to perform a movement or cover a distance in the shortest time.
Test: 50-Meter Sprint, 20-Yard Sprint.

SKILL · 03
Agility

The ability to change direction quickly and with control.
Test: T-Test, Shuttle Run (4x10m).

SKILL · 04
Coordination

The ability to use the senses together with body parts to perform movements smoothly and accurately.
Test: Wall Toss Test, Alternate Hand Test.

SKILL · 05
Reaction Time

The ability to respond quickly to a stimulus.
Test: Ruler Drop Test, Reaction Time App/Device Test.

SKILL · 06
Balance

The ability to maintain the body's center of gravity over its base of support.
Test: Stork Stand Test, Balance Beam Walk.

CHAPTER 03
Summary & Application

All 11 components work together for a genuinely fit and healthy body — health-related components keep you well; skill-related components make movement efficient. Both groups matter for the semester's practical tests.

Tips to Improve Your Fitness

1
Be physically active every day
Consistency matters more than intensity for building baseline fitness.
2
Follow a balanced diet
Supports body composition and recovery.
3
Get enough sleep and rest
Essential for muscular recovery and mental health.
4
Stay hydrated
Affects endurance and reaction performance.
5
Set goals and be consistent
Structured goals drive measurable improvement across components.
6
Track your progress
Retesting the same component tests over time shows real gains.
7
Have fun and enjoy your activities
Sustainability beats short bursts of intensity.

Physical fitness improves health, performance, and quality of life · Regular physical activity prevents hypokinetic diseases · Know and be able to explain all 11 components · Get tested, know your fitness level, and keep improving.
