CHAPTER 04
Family, Women, Schools, Professions & Works Timeline

Rizal was born on June 19, 1861, in Calamba, Laguna, to a prosperous family.

A. Family & Key People

Parents:

  • Francisco Mercado Rizal — his father, a well-to-do sugar planter and landowner.
  • Teodora Alonso Realonda — his mother, one of the best-educated women of her time and Rizal’s first teacher.

Siblings: he was the seventh of 11 children — Saturnina, Paciano (his only brother and closest confidant), Narcisa, Olympia, Lucia, Maria, Concepcion, Josefa, Trinidad, and Soledad.

Key people:

  • Paciano Rizal — his older brother, who acted as his guardian and supported his studies abroad. Paciano was deeply influenced by the GOMBURZA and passed on his nationalist sentiments to José.
  • Ferdinand Blumentritt — an Austrian professor and Rizal’s close friend and pen pal. They corresponded for years before finally meeting in person.

B. The Women in Rizal’s Life

Segunda Katigbak
His first love, a young woman from Lipa, Batangas.
Leonor Valenzuela
A neighbor in Manila, known for their “invisible” love letters written in invisible ink.
Leonor Rivera
Often considered his true love and the inspiration for Maria Clara in Noli Me Tangere. Engaged for many years, until her mother intercepted Rizal’s letters and encouraged her to marry another man.
Consuelo Ortiga y Rey
Courted while in Madrid, but he backed off out of respect for his friend Eduardo de Lete, who was also in love with her.
Seiko Usui (O-Sei-San)
A Japanese woman he fell in love with during his stay in Japan — a brief but intense romance.
Gertrude Beckett
A young woman in London, affectionately called “Getty,” one of his admirers.
Suzanne Jacoby
A Belgian woman he had a relationship with while staying in Brussels.
Nellie Boustead
A rich and beautiful woman from Biarritz. Close to marriage, but it fell through over religious differences and her mother’s disapproval.
Josephine Bracken
An Irish woman who became his common-law wife, brought to him in Dapitan for her stepfather’s eye treatment. Stayed with him until his execution; they married just hours before it.

C. Schools Attended

  • Early education (Biñan, Laguna): studied under Justiniano Aquino Cruz.
  • Ateneo Municipal de Manila: earned a degree as a land surveyor and assessor.
  • University of Santo Tomas (UST): initially studied Philosophy and Letters, then Medicine.
  • Universidad Central de Madrid: obtained his Licentiate in Medicine in 1884, then studied Ophthalmology in Paris (under Dr. Louis de Wecker) and Heidelberg (under Dr. Otto Becker).

D. Professions

Rizal was a polymath whose expertise spanned many fields. He was an:

  • Ophthalmologist (eye specialist) — specialized in ophthalmology to cure his mother’s eye ailment.
  • Writer/novelist — author of Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo.
  • Poet — wrote numerous poems, including his farewell masterpiece, Mi Último Adiós.
  • Sculptor & painter — a talented artist who studied sculpture and painting.
  • Other fields: architect, educator, economist, ethnologist, farmer, historian, inventor, journalist, linguist (knew 22 languages), musician, mythologist, naturalist, sociologist, and more.

E. Major Works & Timelines

WorkTypeTimeline & Details
Noli Me TangereNovelPublished in Berlin, Germany, 1887 — a passionate exposure of the injustices of Spanish rule and the abuses of the friars.
El FilibusterismoNovelPublished in Ghent, Belgium, 1891 — sequel to the Noli, exploring themes of revenge and revolution. Dedicated to GOMBURZA.
Mi Último AdiósPoemWritten on the eve of his execution, December 29, 1896, in his Fort Santiago cell. Title translates to “My Last Farewell.”

F. Key Events: Before & After Dapitan

Before Dapitan (1887–1892)

  • 1887: Rizal returned to the Philippines after his first trip abroad but was forced to leave again due to opposition from the Spanish authorities regarding Noli Me Tangere.
  • 1888–1892: lived in Hong Kong and Macau practicing medicine, then traveled to Japan, the United States, and Europe. Continued writing and founded La Liga Filipina upon his return to Manila in July 1892.

Dapitan exile (1892–1896)

  • July 6, 1892: arrested shortly after founding La Liga Filipina and deported to Dapitan, Mindanao, for four years.
  • Life in Dapitan: practiced ophthalmology, taught locals, improved agriculture, engaged in scientific work, and met and fell in love with Josephine Bracken.

After Dapitan & execution (1896)

  • July 31, 1896: left Dapitan for Manila, hoping to travel to Cuba as a volunteer doctor — but the revolution had already begun.
  • October 6, 1896: arrested and imprisoned in Fort Santiago, Manila.
  • December 30, 1896: executed by firing squad at Bagumbayan (now Luneta). He married Josephine Bracken in his cell just before his execution.

  4. TRUE — The law emphasizes developing nationalism and patriotism.

  8. TRUE — It was approved on June 12, 1956.

Exam Strategy & Traps
FOR MULTIPLE CHOICE
Work the options

1. Read all options before answering
2. Watch for “NOT” and “EXCEPT”
3. Dates are frequently tested — memorize the key ones
4. Names are frequently tested — know who did what
5. Eliminate obviously wrong answers first

FOR IDENTIFICATION
Spelling counts

1. Practice writing names correctly
2. If you don’t know the exact answer, write what you know
3. Common answers: Rizal Law, Recto, Laurel, Magsaysay, Viola, Blumentritt

For Modified True or False

If FALSE, you must both identify the wrong word and supply the correction. Common traps: wrong dates, wrong names, wrong titles.
